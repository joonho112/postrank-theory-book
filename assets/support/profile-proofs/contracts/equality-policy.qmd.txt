## Status and structural identity {#sec-p2-equality-policy}

This Step 3.4 contract supplements [reporting semantics](reporting-semantics.qmd) and [core loss](loss-core.qmd). The weak-error loss remains primary. The neutral branch below is an explicitly different decision specification, adapted from P1's tie-aware loss; it is not a changed interpretation of the core risk. The [P1 correspondence](../evidence/p2-v1/p1-correspondence.qmd#sec-inheritance-equality) records the inheritance boundary.

For each domain, declare an equivalence relation $E_d$ on the units, with scientific or model provenance for each equality class. Its meaning is a target identity: whenever $iE_dj$, the two domain targets are the same quantity throughout the declared target/model state space. For example, sites sharing a community can inherit an identical community target while retaining distinct site-composition targets. Require compatibility of the supplied law:

$$
\Pi_s\{\Theta:\theta_{id}=\theta_{jd}\text{ for every }d\text{ and }iE_dj\mid y\}=1.
$$

Restrict admissible reports by

$$
iE_dj\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g_{id}=g_{jd}.
$$

These are hard reporting guards, including in zero-loss-weight domains. Incompatible law/guard metadata must be resolved before evaluation; do not silently average unequal targets, delete states, or discard a declared guard. The all-withheld action respects every guard. Under the core loss, within-class withheld pairs still incur their constant $\tau_d/M$ domain contribution; later compression must preserve multiplicities and constants.

The guard is a declared restriction. No claim is made that imposing it preserves an optimum of an unguarded problem with joint constraints, grade caps, or other restrictions. P1's equality-neutral guard theorem has narrower assumptions and does not supply that claim.

## Equality evidence and domain specificity {#sec-p2-equality-evidence}

Distinguish three situations. A declared structural identity supplies the preceding guard. Positive posterior equality mass without such an identity remains ordinary uncertainty: a disclosed strict assertion fails at equality under the core loss. Equal values in all saved Monte Carlo states do not establish probability-one equality under the intended posterior, much less scientific structural identity. Rounding and numerical tolerances do not change these statements.

If a finite support and its weights define the **exact supplied law**, equality at every positive-mass support point does establish probability-one equality under that law. This mathematical fact should be recorded as such. It is not automatically scientific provenance for a structural target identity under another intended model; adopting a guard still requires the declared identity and its scope. Approximate finite support must remain labeled approximate.

Relations belong to domains. For three units, let $E_1$ have classes $\{1,2\},\{3\}$ and $E_2$ have $\{1\},\{2,3\}$. The compatible state and guarded report

$$
\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\3&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad G=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\\2&2\end{pmatrix}
$$

retain two grades in each domain and three occupied profiles. Intersecting the relations globally would miss the required within-domain ties. Imposing their union's transitive closure in every domain would suppress permissible distinctions. A common-grade diagonal must, however, satisfy both guards on its one shared column; in this example that forces all three units into one grade. This follows from the specified shared action, not from replacing the domain relations in the product family.

## Neutral loss and its interpretation {#sec-p2-neutral-loss}

Retain $x_{ij}^d$, $N_d$ and $M$ from the core definitions. Define strict-reversal and distinct-target counts

$$
F_d^{\mathrm{rev}}=\sum_{i\ne j}x_{ij}^d\mathbf1\{\theta_{id}<\theta_{jd}\},
\qquad
N_d^{\ne}=\sum_{i\ne j}x_{ij}^d\mathbf1\{\theta_{id}\ne\theta_{jd}\},
$$

$$
W_d^{\ne}=\sum_{i<j}\mathbf1\{g_{id}=g_{jd}\}\mathbf1\{\theta_{id}\ne\theta_{jd}\}.
$$

The optional neutral loss is

$$
L_d^{\mathrm{neu}}=\frac{F_d^{\mathrm{rev}}+\tau_d W_d^{\ne}}{M},
\quad L_{\mathrm{neu}}=\sum_dw_dL_d^{\mathrm{neu}},
\quad R_{\mathrm{neu}}=E_{\Pi_s}[L_{\mathrm{neu}}\mid y].
$$

All possible pairs still form the loss denominator. At latent equality **all three actions cost zero**, including withholding. At distinct targets the neutral and core pair costs agree. The all-withheld neutral risk is

$$
\frac1M\sum_dw_d\tau_d\sum_{i<j}\Pi_s(\theta_{id}\ne\theta_{jd}\mid y),
$$

which need not equal the core baseline $\sum_dw_d\tau_d$.

A neutral error guarantee concerns **no strict reversal**, not strict correctness. Equality makes an issued strict assertion false under the reporting semantics even when its neutral cost is zero. A display must name the weaker guarantee; zero neutral loss is not evidence that all displayed strict inequalities hold. This branch does not introduce an equality assertion or practical-equivalence margin.

## Three denominator choices {#sec-p2-neutral-denominators}

For fixed $G$, $N_d$ is deterministic, whereas $N_d^{\ne}$ depends on the target state. Always $0\le F_d^{\mathrm{rev}}\le N_d^{\ne}\le N_d$. The following summaries are different:

1. $E[F_d^{\mathrm{rev}}\mid y]/N_d$, for $N_d>0$, counts reversals among all reported pairs, retaining equal-target reports in the denominator.
2. $E[F_d^{\mathrm{rev}}\mid y]/E[N_d^{\ne}\mid y]$, for positive expected distinct mass, is a ratio of expectations.
3. $E[\widetilde Q_d^{\ne}\mid y]$, where $\widetilde Q_d^{\ne}=F_d^{\mathrm{rev}}/N_d^{\ne}$ if $N_d^{\ne}>0$ and zero otherwise, averages a statewise active-pair fraction.

Zero denominators display as unavailable/no active comparisons. The zero extension in item 3 is computational, not perfect accuracy. Conditioning item 3 on $N_d^{\ne}>0$ produces yet another quantity. If $E[N_d^{\ne}]=0$, nonnegativity forces both active and reversal counts to vanish almost surely, but item 2 remains undefined.

For $t\in[0,1]$, tail events likewise require a denominator choice: $\{F_d^{\mathrm{rev}}>tN_d\}$ and $\{F_d^{\mathrm{rev}}>tN_d^{\ne}\}$ are distinct. The latter uses a statewise random denominator and has no exceedance in its empty state. Neither event becomes a core weak-error guarantee by renaming it. Scoped or joint versions require their own declared masks, thresholds and union event; none is silently activated here.

## Exact denominator fixture {#sec-p2-equality-fixtures}

Take $n=3$, one domain and $g=(1,2,2)$, so $N=2$. The exact supplied law is:

| Target state | Mass | $F^{\mathrm{rev}}$ | $N^{\ne}$ | $\widetilde Q^{\ne}$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $(1,2,1)$ | $1/3$ | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| $(3,1,2)$ | $1/2$ | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| $(0,0,0)$ | $1/6$ | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Thus $E[F^{\mathrm{rev}}]=1/3$, $E[N^{\ne}]=4/3$ and the three summaries are respectively $1/6$, $1/4$ and $1/3$. Conditional on positive active count, the last becomes $2/5$. These differences are exact, not Monte Carlo error.

[The fixtures](../fixtures/p2-v1/equality-cases.json) also check all nine core/neutral pair costs, positive equality mass, and crossing domain identities. They include hand-readable expected values and an embedded executable Python checker using exact rational arithmetic. This validates the examples, not a reference evaluator, optimizer, or preservation theorem.
