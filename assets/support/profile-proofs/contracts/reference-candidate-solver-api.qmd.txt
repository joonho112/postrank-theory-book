## Export and optimization scope {#sec-candidate-solver-export}

Step 7.2 implements [solve-candidates.R](../R/profile/solve-candidates.R):

```r
p2_solve_candidates(
  input, family, decision = NULL, objective = "A",
  protection = "direct_joint", utility = NULL, max_actions = 100000L,
  construction_input = NULL, evaluation_relationship = NULL
)
```

Source the input, action, decision, expected, joint and candidate-bank modules
before the solver; summary construction also uses the summary validator.
Dependencies resolve in the function's caller/source environment. Isolated
environments with `baseenv()` ancestry additionally provide `stats::setNames`,
as in the reference loader convention. No shared loader is modified by this
Step.

The input is a validated `p2_profile_input`; family is a validated
`p2_candidate_family` from the [candidate constructors](candidate-family.qmd).
The solver examines **every** member of that declared product. It does not
expand its banks, infer a target order, or claim optimality over all ordered
partitions. It supports $n>4$ when the declared product remains bounded. An exact
integer product-size check against `max_actions` precedes domain precomputation
and product enumeration. Exceeding the limit raises an explicit refusal; there
is no silent truncation, partial solution or infeasibility declaration.

Objective A minimizes additive expected error–withholding risk. Objective B
maximizes separately declared pair-domain utility $I_v$. Utility accepts the
same nonnegative numeric/rational-text $M\times D$ policy and pair/domain
ordering as the [small oracle](reference-oracle-api.qmd). Numeric entries mean
their stored binary rationals; default utility is one per disclosed statement.
Neither objective uses grade or cell counts as its score. All optional mean
constraints and the selected protection rule remain active for zero-weight
domains.

## Immutable construction and new evaluation inputs {#sec-candidate-solver-compatibility}

The solver verifies the family hash after removing its own hash/ID fields,
then validates its bank hashes and reconstructs only the family wrapper from
the original banks. The result must exactly match the supplied family. It never
rebuilds or relabels a bank's construction provenance using evaluation samples.

If `p2_hash(input$original_spec)` matches the construction source hash,
`construction_input` may be omitted and the relationship defaults to
`same_input`. Otherwise callers must supply the original validated
`construction_input` and an explicit list:

```r
list(
  relation = "new_payload_same_declared_law",
  same_target_law_asserted = TRUE,
  justification = "Reason the intended target and conditional law agree",
  sample_relationship = "How construction and evaluation samples relate"
)
```

The original input's hash must match the family's construction binding. The
solver requires identical unit/domain IDs, complete `target_spec` records
(including orientations and transformations), and equality guards. It also
checks intended-law `identifier`, `producer_class`, `model_version` and
`conditioning_info`, plus provenance `conditioning_data_id` and
`conditioning_data_hash`. Payload hashes, state identifiers, sample lengths
and representations may differ. A same-input call cannot describe itself as a
new payload, and a changed-input call cannot use the same-input label.

These are mechanical checks of **declared compatibility**, not verification
that two generators actually target the same probability law. An explicit
new-input justification is retained. State labels or changed sample lengths do
not prove disjointness or independence. Heldout inputs are permitted, but this
solver does not certify that they are held out. Original construction flags,
draw IDs, artifact hashes and provenance remain unchanged. The solver also
records that its selected action uses the evaluation measure; reuse for search
does not constitute independent validation.

## Domain precomputation and joint combination {#sec-candidate-solver-precomputation}

For each bank column, the solver constructs an action with that column in its
domain and all other domains withheld. It calls the existing
[joint evaluator](reference-joint-api.qmd), which calls expected evaluation and
revalidates the action. Thus weak-error comparisons, original $M$, scoped counts,
strict integer cutoffs and mean constraints come from the established modules.
The precomputation retains the selected domain's weighted loss contribution,
mean-constraint rows, event probabilities, and original-state failure union.
It does **not** add the complete one-column action's risk across domains, which
would duplicate other domains' withholding constants.

Each product combines its domain contributions additively. For direct
protection, it ORs the selected domain failure masks on their shared original
state basis. Multiple active domains require actual joint capability and a
joint payload with identical payload ID, state IDs/history, representation,
weights, retention and sampling metadata. No sorting or row alignment creates
coupling. A single active domain can use its own criterionwise full-vector
states. An empty or analytically inactive tail has probability zero, including
valid summary mean-only problems. Nontrivial scalar-summary tails are rejected.

The alternative selectors preserve the same family, risk and mean constraints:
`bonferroni` requires $\sum_h p_h\le\alpha$;
`equal_bonferroni` requires every $p_h\le\alpha/H$. Here $H$ is the complete
configured row count, including inactive rows. At $H=0$ protection is explicitly
disabled and vacuous. Conservative values are not clipped or mislabeled as
joint probabilities. If any required precomputation or product cannot be
evaluated, the whole call fails with an informative error. Such candidates are
not silently discarded or assigned infeasible status.

## Returned objects and selection {#sec-candidate-solver-return}

The result has class `p2_candidate_solution` and contains `candidate_table`,
`actions`, `event_tables`, `optimizer_ids`, `selected_id`, `selected_action`,
`domain_precomputation`, unchanged `family`, `decision`, `objective`,
`protection`, `utility`, and `receipts`.

Candidate rows follow lexicographic canonical-$G$ order: domain-major, with
units in their declared order. Action IDs are content hashes of IDs and
canonical $G$, so they need not equal the small oracle's sequential IDs.
`actions` and `event_tables` are named by those hashes. The full optimizer set
is retained; the first optimizer in the declared lexicographic order is selected
without a secondary objective. Hash identity is not an assertion of numerical
or scientific validity.

The candidate table uses the oracle's numeric fields `risk`, `J`,
`protection_value`, `Iv`, `I`, `C`, exact-character counterparts where available,
and logical `mean_feasible`, `protection_feasible`,
`feasible_for_represented_measure`, `optimal`, `selected`. Named domain counts
appear in list columns `K` and `N`. `status` states arithmetic precision.
For conservative protection, `J` and `J_exact` remain `NA`; the protection value
is respectively the probability sum or $H$ times the maximum marginal event
probability. Empty protection has value zero.

`domain_precomputation[[domain_id]][[candidate_id]]` includes `grades`, the
single-row `domain_table`, domain-only `expected_constraints` and `event_table`,
`mean_feasible`, `active_event`, `failure_trace`, `Iv`/`Iv_exact`, `N`, `K` and
`expected_arithmetic`. Active failure traces retain the joint evaluator's
original history, weights and `union_event`; inactive columns have a `NULL`
trace because their failure is analytically false. Domain precomputation count
is the sum of bank sizes, while `examined_actions` is their product.

Receipts preserve family/bank construction records, separate construction and
evaluation input hashes, payload histories and receipts, the relationship
assertion, exact product size, examined/precomputed counts, timing, decision
hash and hashes of the solver/evaluator function forms and bodies. These last
hashes are **function-content hashes**, not source-file hashes; the test receipt
records actual file hashes. The receipt distinguishes a restricted exact optimum
for the represented measure from an optimum of computed approximate values.

## Arithmetic, assurance and actual checks {#sec-candidate-solver-checks}

Rational finite masses and equal-weight empirical measures preserve exact
domain contributions, utility and event probabilities. Their objective and
feasibility comparisons use rational arithmetic. Floating expectations or
probabilities retain approximate qualifiers; approximate objective comparison
uses the actual floating values without an epsilon tie rule. Complete search
does not remove approximation error. Exact empirical optimization is not an
intended-posterior certificate, even when a fixed candidate bank was declared.
No sampling independence, mixing, selection-adjustment or generator correctness
is established here.

The focused command `Rscript scripts/p2_test_profile.R candidate-solver` passed
**10 blocks and 148 expectations** at 2026-11-05 22:15:48 UTC, with no failures,
errors, warnings or skips. The [machine receipt](../results/p2-v1/tests/reference-tests-candidate-solver.json)
records the run and source hashes. Tests compare complete seed banks with the
full oracle at $n=2,3,4$, all A/B and protection combinations, exact utility,
restricted banks, mean and zero-weight protection, integrity failures,
construction/evaluation compatibility, different-length empirical payloads,
criterionwise information limits, summaries and floating arithmetic. An
$n=40$ case verifies 14 precomputations and 49 examined products. An additional
three-state $n=3,D=2$ law with latent equality, overlapping scopes, distinct
positive thresholds and mean caps verifies all 169 products against the full
oracle under all three protection rules. Its three configured events include
two in the first domain, exercising within-domain union before cross-domain
mask combination; exact risks, event/union probabilities, feasibility and the
complete optimizer sets agree.

Root review caught an inherited R dependency-lookup hazard in an isolated
environment; capturing the function evaluation environment repaired it, and an
actual `baseenv()`-parent loading regression now passes. A failed initial error
message assertion was corrected to match the explicit refusal semantics.
These are implementation checks, not external review or numerical assurance.
