## Exports and family construction {#sec-oracle-exports}

Step 6.6 implements two functions in [oracle.R](../R/profile/oracle.R):

```r
p2_enumerate_columns(n, equality_classes = NULL, grade_cap = n)
p2_oracle(input, decision = NULL, objective = "A", family = "product",
          protection = "direct_joint", grade_caps = NULL,
          utility = NULL, max_actions = 100000L)
```

Source the input, action, decision, expected and joint modules first; constructing
summary inputs additionally needs the summary validator. The oracle consumes
validated objects under their existing contracts. It supports $2\le n\le4$,
with arbitrary positive $D$ subject to the explicit action-count limit.

`p2_enumerate_columns()` returns an integer matrix whose rows are canonical
ordered partitions, sorted lexicographically in unit order. Columns are
positional units, labeled `unit-1`, etc.; rows are `column-001`, etc.
Its optional class vector is positional: names carry no additional mapping.
All vectors in $\{1,\ldots,n\}^n$ are considered, retaining precisely those
whose occupied labels are $1,\ldots,K$, whose $K$ respects the cap, and which
do not split an equality class. Therefore every admissible ordered partition
appears exactly once. Unguarded counts are **3, 13 and 75** for $n=2,3,4$.
No target mean, fixed order or adjacent-merge restriction is used.

The full oracle checks actual unit/domain IDs and guards through the action and
evaluation APIs. Grade caps require one value per domain, with any names in the
exact declared order. `family = "product"` enumerates every product of allowed
domain columns. `family = "common"` intersects the allowed column sets across
all domains and repeats each remaining column. This enforces every guard and
cap, including crossing identities, on the diagonal family. All-withheld is
present in either family. The exact integer family size is compared to
`max_actions` before allocating or evaluating its product. Exceeding the cap
raises an error; it does not trigger truncation, sampling or a partial-search
claim. The scalar limit must be a positive R-range integer.

## Objectives, utility and protection {#sec-oracle-decision}

The oracle uses [the declared decision](profile-risk-spec.qmd) without modifying
its loss weights, withholding costs, scopes, mean constraints or thresholds.
Objective `"A"` minimizes additive expected risk. Objective `"B"` maximizes the
separately declared
$I_v=\sum_{d,i<j}v_{ij}^d\mathbf1\{g_{id}\ne g_{jd}\}$ on the same feasible
set. B introduces no secondary risk objective or additional risk cap.

`utility = NULL` supplies unit weights. Otherwise, utility is a nonnegative
numeric or rational-text $M\times D$ matrix with finite numeric projections.
Rows follow `selected_action$pair_index`; any row names must be `pair-1`, etc.,
and any column names must exactly match domain IDs. Numeric entries denote
their stored binary rational values; text such as `"1/3"` denotes that exact
fraction. Utility is evaluated with exact rational arithmetic and is distinct
from grade counts, occupied cells and loss weights. The returned `utility`
list retains numeric `values`, canonical-text `exact`, `encoding` and
`pair_index`. All numeric outputs are projections; exact fields remain
authoritative when a value cannot be represented exactly as a double.

`protection` selects one of three matched rules. Let $H$ be the **configured**
number of protection rows and $p_h$ each corresponding strict-exceedance
probability:

| Selector | Constraint | Returned `protection_value` |
|---|---|---|
| `direct_joint` | $J\le\alpha$ | Union probability $J$ |
| `bonferroni` | $\sum_h p_h\le\alpha$ | $\sum_h p_h$ |
| `equal_bonferroni` | Every $p_h\le\alpha/H$ | $H\max_h p_h$ |

All three also enforce every optional mean constraint, including those on
zero-loss-weight domains. $H=0$ is explicitly disabled and vacuous protection,
with protection value zero. Inactive rows remain in the configured $H$ for
equal allocation. Bounds are not clipped to one. Bonferroni values are not
reported as actual joint probabilities.

Direct evaluation invokes the [joint evaluator](reference-joint-api.qmd) on
the complete event table. The two conservative alternatives invoke that same
evaluator separately for each event, preserving exact thresholds and parameters.
Consequently they can use criterionwise full-vector states without pairing
rows across payloads. A summary supports only the analytic-event cases already
supported by the evaluator. If any candidate cannot be evaluated under the
requested rule, the oracle rejects the entire search with its candidate ID and
reason. Missing information is never labeled infeasibility or used to discard
a candidate silently.

## Complete results and tie-breaking {#sec-oracle-return}

The `p2_oracle_result` contains:

- `candidate_table`: one row for every candidate, keyed by `action_id`;
- `actions`: named canonical $G$ matrices keyed by the same IDs;
- `event_tables`: matching per-candidate protection-row tables;
- `optimizer_ids`, `selected_id`, and normalized `selected_action`;
- unchanged `decision`, declared `utility`, `family`, `objective` and `protection`;
- `receipts` describing the full family and qualification of the result.

Candidate IDs are `action-000001`, etc., in lexicographic canonical-$G$ order:
first domain, its units in declared order, then subsequent domains. This order
is also the declared deterministic tie-break. The complete optimizer set is
computed **before** selecting its first ID; no other objective breaks ties.
The all-withheld ID is retained explicitly in the receipt.

`candidate_table` includes numeric `risk`, `J`, `protection_value`, `Iv`, `I`
and `C`, with canonical-text counterparts `risk_exact`, `J_exact`,
`protection_value_exact` and `Iv_exact`. `I` is the unweighted disclosure count.
List columns `K` and `N` retain named per-domain grade and disclosure counts.
Logical columns are `mean_feasible`, `protection_feasible`,
`feasible_for_represented_measure`, `optimal` and `selected`; `status` records
exact versus approximate represented-measure arithmetic. For conservative
comparators, `J` and `J_exact` are `NA`, even if a separate direct calculation
could be performed. Unavailable exact table entries are `NA_character_`.
Empty conservative event tables retain the empty decision table; nonempty
tables preserve configured row order and sequential `joint-1`, etc., IDs.

Receipts include family size and column counts, caps and equality classes,
the explicit limit, globality and tie-break statements, protection meaning and
configured event count, arithmetic and objective-comparison status, original
input/selection receipts, the decision configuration hash, UTC start/end times
and elapsed seconds. Original selection metadata describes the producer's
input; it is not a claim that this optimizer was chosen without inspecting
that measure. The oracle selects using the evaluated measure and performs no
held-out validation.

## Arithmetic, assurance and verification {#sec-oracle-validation}

Rational finite and equal-weight empirical inputs retain exact risks and event
probabilities, so feasibility and objective comparisons use `bigq` values.
Floating masses and numeric summaries carry approximate labels. Approximate
risk comparisons use their floating values without a tie tolerance; their
returned optimizer set is conditional on those computed values. A finite
enumeration of that approximate objective does not establish exact posterior
optimality. Exact empirical optimization likewise supplies no intended-posterior,
Monte Carlo, iid, mixing or sample-adaptive confidence certificate.

Globality means only completion of the stated guarded/capped ordered-partition
family at $n\le4$. It excludes free-comparison actions, unimplemented candidate
families and large-scale optimization. The [bundle evaluator](reference-bundle-api.qmd)
can be called separately on a selected action; the oracle enables no bundle
objective or constraint.
