## Decision and truth conventions {#sec-p2-core-loss}

The primary loss evaluates the strict directions defined in [reporting semantics](reporting-semantics.qmd). It penalizes a false issued direction by one and a withheld pair by a declared cost $\tau_d$. There is no latent equality claim in a grade tie. The canonical definitions are in [elementary loss](../proofs/definitions/elementary-loss.qmd). This is a decision specification adapted from the P1 error–withholding formulation. Formal reduction and sufficiency results are reserved for Phase 4.

The baseline uses **weak direction error**: an issued statement $\theta_{id}>\theta_{jd}$ fails when $\theta_{id}\leq\theta_{jd}$. This includes equality, not just strict reversal. For one unordered pair the statewise costs are:

| Report in domain $d$ | $\theta_{id}>\theta_{jd}$ | $\theta_{id}=\theta_{jd}$ | $\theta_{id}<\theta_{jd}$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $g_{id}<g_{jd}$ | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| $g_{id}=g_{jd}$ | $\tau_d$ | $\tau_d$ | $\tau_d$ |
| $g_{id}>g_{jd}$ | 1 | 1 | 0 |

Both possible strict directions therefore fail at latent equality. Equal target values in one posterior state do not retroactively change the disclosure count or the grade action. An equality-neutral rule is a different loss and will receive its own event/denominator contract.

## Count once, then normalize {#sec-p2-loss-counting}

Let $M=\binom n2>0$. There are $M$ possible unordered unit pairs in each domain, not $n(n-1)$ independent comparisons. Summing over ordered indices is safe only because at most one of $x_{ij}^d$ and $x_{ji}^d$ can be one. Define $N_d$ as the number of disclosed pairs and $F_d$ as the number of their false directions. Then $0\leq F_d\leq N_d\leq M$, and $M-N_d$ pairs are withheld.

The domain loss divides $F_d+\tau_d(M-N_d)$ by **all** possible pairs. It is not the fraction of errors among disclosed pairs. Dividing the latter by $N_d$ gives a different functional and requires an empty-case convention. Counts for protected scopes are introduced separately in Step 3.5; they do not replace $M$ in this core objective.

Loss weights satisfy $w_d\geq0$ and $\sum_dw_d=1$; withholding costs satisfy $0<\tau_d\leq1/2$. All are declared and fixed in the posterior evaluation. A zero loss weight removes that domain's contribution to the objective, but does not remove any separately declared protection in that domain. If several actions are optimal, a solver must record its tie-breaking rule; these definitions do not grant a unique optimum or a maximum-disclosure solution.

## Expected loss and what it requires {#sec-p2-loss-expectation}

The primary risk is the expectation of the weighted domain loss under the supplied law. Boundedness ensures that this expectation exists even when target means do not. An all-withheld matrix has risk $\sum_dw_d\tau_d$. This remains true when a structural guard forces some pairs to be withheld: the baseline counts their constant withholding cost. Removing those pairs or renormalizing changes the declared objective unless the omitted constant and weights are handled explicitly.

Write $e_{ij}^d=\Pi_s(\theta_{id}\leq\theta_{jd}\mid y)$. For a fixed matrix,

$$
R_{\mathrm{sep}}(G)
=\frac1M\sum_{d=1}^D w_d
\left[\sum_{i\ne j}x_{ij}^d(G)e_{ij}^d+
\tau_d\{M-N_d(G)\}\right].
$$

This identity uses finite linearity of expectation, not independence. If equality has positive probability, $e_{ij}^d+e_{ji}^d=1+\Pi_s(\theta_{id}=\theta_{jd}\mid y)$, so the two weak-error probabilities need not be complements. Marginal quantities sufficient to evaluate this additive expectation do not in general identify the later joint violation event.

Domain weights price reporting consequences. They never multiply target magnitudes to create a composite need score. Withholding cost prices incomplete reporting; it is not an error-rate cap. Optional expected-FDP budgets, realized-FDP thresholds and posterior joint-error probability budgets will have different symbols and roles.

## Fixed specification and boundedness {#sec-p2-loss-boundaries}

With $0<\tau_d\leq1/2$, each pair cost lies in $[0,1]$, hence $0\leq L_{\mathrm{sep}}\leq1$ and $0\leq R_{\mathrm{sep}}\leq1$. The upper bound does not describe frequentist calibration or Monte Carlo error. Numerical estimates, exact finite-law evaluations and intended-posterior risks must retain their distinct statuses.

The core has no practical superiority margin, random denominator, magnitude-dependent loss, or randomized report averaging. Such alternatives are meaningful only with an explicit revised contract. In particular, replacing $\leq$ by a floating-point tolerance changes the scientific error event; a tolerance for checking numerical feasibility is not permission to do so.
